THE STARTING POINT

Survival mode.

What can survival mode look like?

  • functioning on autopilot
  • constantly putting out fires
  • ignoring your own needs
  • difficulty planning beyond the immediate future
  • making decisions primarily from fear
  • staying busy to avoid emotions
  • remaining in unhealthy situations because they feel familiar
  • feeling emotionally disconnected
  • constantly expecting something to go wrong
  • believing, “This is just how my life is.”

How do you get out?

OPENING REFLECTION

STAGE 1 · PRECONTEMPLATION – CONTEMPLATION

Recognizing survival mode.

Precontemplation

A person may not yet recognize that something needs to change. They may believe:

  • This is normal
  • I’m fine
  • This is just who I am
  • I’m handling it

Contemplation

A person begins to recognize that something may not be working. They begin asking: “Is this healthy?” “Is this really how I want to live?” “Could something be different?”

This stage centers on:

  • awareness
  • insight
  • pattern recognition
  • increasing motivation for change

STAGE 2 · PREPARATION TO ACTION

Building a plan.

Adaptive coping

Adaptive coping refers to thoughts, behaviors, or strategies that help a person adjust to or manage difficult circumstances. Examples may include:

  • becoming highly independent when support was unavailable
  • emotionally distancing during overwhelming situations
  • becoming highly alert in unpredictable environments
  • working excessively to maintain stability
  • trying to reduce conflict in unsafe situations

STEP 3 · ACTION – MAINTENANCE

I’m out. Now what?

Maladaptive coping

Maladaptive coping refers to thoughts, behaviors, or strategies that may provide short-term relief or protection but eventually interfere with healthy functioning, relationships, healing, or growth.

EXAMPLES

  • hyper-independence
  • people-pleasing
  • emotional shutdown
  • avoidance
  • hypervigilance
  • overfunctioning
  • difficulty trusting
  • constantly expecting danger
  • inability to receive support

ACTION → MAINTENANCE

What helped me survive may not be what helps me thrive. What no longer works?

Practice. Maintain.

  1. Recognize the old survival pattern
  2. Replace it with a healthier response
  3. Practice the new response consistently
  4. Maintain it through triggers, setbacks, and real-life stress

PART 4

Rebuilding me.

Rebuilding is not necessarily returning to who you were before the difficult season. Sometimes rebuilding means becoming someone new.

Participants begin exploring:

  • Who am I now?
  • Who am I becoming?
  • What matters to me?
  • What are my values?
  • What relationships support my growth?
  • What boundaries protect the life I am building?
  • What habits support the woman I want to become?
  • What purpose do I want my life to reflect?

CLOSING · FROM SURVIVING TO THRIVING

Surviving got you here.
Thriving is what you build next.

Survival does not have to become your permanent identity. What was adaptive for one season may become maladaptive in another. You can honor the version of yourself that learned how to survive while still allowing God to transform the way you live now.

“God, what kind of life are You calling me to build from here?”
That is where thriving begins.
